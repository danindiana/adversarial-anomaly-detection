# Persistent Nonhuman Cohabitation: A Model-Comparison and Adversarial-Detection Framework

**Version:** 1.1.0  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Status:** Conceptual research note with reproducible *synthetic* calculations; **not** an empirical claim of extraterrestrial residence, detection, or intervention.  
**Companion code:** `verify_models.py` (unchanged v1.0 verification) and `verify_models_v1_1.py` (closed forms, cost sensitivity, Stackelberg game).

## Abstract

Consider four stipulated propositions: (1) non-Earth-origin intelligence has been present for an interval overlapping human evolution; (2) some entities reside on Earth's surface and are difficult to distinguish from humans; (3) the entities generally avoid intentional steering of human development; and (4) they are comparatively indifferent to ordinary human affairs but have interests in global environmental and technological hazards. These stipulations do not establish that any entities exist. They define a space of counterfactual models whose empirical predictions can be examined.

The analysis separates **presence, detectability, objectives, operational roles, and intervention**. It compares a null hypothesis against idealized warden and cohabitant objectives, potentially served by embedded monitors. A hierarchical Poisson–Gamma model is used to illustrate background-limited detection, a time-varying hazard model to explore secrecy constraints, a sampling model to examine genomic detectability, and a threshold model to distinguish intervention motives. Finally, a hypothetical strategic hider chooses its detectability to balance predictive distinguishability against an operating cost. All reported numbers are outputs of specified synthetic priors, not probability estimates about real extraterrestrial agents.

## 1. Hypotheses, variables, and causal separation

- **H0 (null):** no resident nonhuman intelligence; observations are generated by terrestrial mechanisms, measurement error, and ordinary background processes.
- **HW (warden objective):** an intelligence values preservation of a specified terrestrial biological or evolutionary property and may intervene to prevent unacceptable losses.
- **HC (cohabitant objective):** an intelligence values its own local environmental, infrastructural, or other interests and may intervene to protect them.
- **HM (monitoring role):** on-site observation or information collection. This is an *operational role*, not a mutually exclusive motive. Monitoring may serve HW, HC, or other objectives.

Track five distinct variables: resident population `N(t)`, social integration fraction `f(t)`, observation technology and protocol `A(t)`, per-resident detectability `d(t;A,policy)`, and intervention policy `q_H(r,t)`. Reported detections also depend on a background term `B(t;A)`. Increasing reports need not imply increasing presence, and increased presence need not imply intervention.

**Political distinction:** capacity to govern, actual governance, local participation, conditional custodianship, and environmental veto power are different properties. Near-total non-interference is impossible for materially embedded agents in a literal causal sense; a coherent non-intervention rule must instead specify disallowed *intentional steering*, outcomes, or scope.

## 2. Noisy observation channel and Bayesian model comparison

Define a fixed surveillance campaign with observed candidate reports `Y`. Under an ordinary-background model:

\[
B\sim \operatorname{Gamma}(k,\theta),\quad
Y\mid B,N,d\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(B+Nd).
\]

The Gamma parametrization uses **shape** `k=100` and **scale** `theta=10`, so `E[B]=1000` and `SD[B]=100`. Marginally, the background count is negative-binomial (`k=100`, `p=1/11`). The presence model convolves this background predictive distribution with an independent Poisson count of mean `s=Nd`. That is a count model; it does not identify the physical cause of any report.

For an observed `Y=1020`:

| Detection model | Synthetic Bayes factor, presence/null |
|---|---:|
| Known background `B=1000`, fixed signal `s=20` | 1.220 |
| Uncertain Gamma background, fixed signal `s=20` | 1.037 |
| Uncertain background; `N∈{100,1000,10000}` and `d∈{0.001,0.01}`, equally weighted | 0.979 |

With background uncertainty of 100, a nominal excess of 20 reports provides essentially no discrimination. Independent background calibration matters more than enlarging an undifferentiated catalog of reports.

### High-specificity comparison with matched uncertainty treatment

Set `Y=2` and null background mean `0.05`. For a sharp alternative signal `s=2`, the Bayes factor is **227.499** if background is known and **125.185** if background follows a Gamma prior with mean and standard deviation both equal to 0.05. For the **explicitly specified** log-uniform alternative `s~LogUniform(0.01,100)`, the Bayes factor becomes **48.165** (known background) or **26.635** (uncertain background). A claim like “log-uniform across four decades yields BF≈60” is incomplete without stating the four-decade interval and background model.

Likelihood ratios favoring a broad anomalous alternative do not identify an extraterrestrial origin. A real experiment would need independent controls, data provenance, contamination assessment, prospective selection rules, and competing natural/technical mechanisms.

## 3. Secrecy and effective historical time

For independent agents, a per-agent leakage hazard `lambda(t)` and probability `v(t)` of a leak becoming verifiable:

\[
P(L=0)=\exp\!\left[-\int_0^T N(t)\lambda(t)v(t)\,dt\right].
\]

Consider 100 years with `N(t)=100+9t`, `v(t)=0.01+0.0009t` and `0≤t≤100`. Then `∫N(t)dt=55,000` agent-years and `∫N(t)v(t)dt=3,700` verification-weighted agent-years. With an *assumed raw leakage* rate `lambda=0.001`, `P(no verified leak)=exp(-3.7)=2.472%`. If instead `lambda~LogUniform(1e-5,1e-2)`, the prior-predictive probability is approximately **39.9%**. These figures are extremely prior-sensitive.

**Important correction:** Grimes (2016) published a correction specifying an integrated hazard for time-varying participant populations—the same form used above. Grimes (2016) estimates an annual per-participant probability of secrecy-ending **exposure**, approximately `4.09×10^-6` in a best-case illustrative calibration, not merely an otherwise invisible leak. Treating this as `lambda` and multiplying by `v(t)` again can double-count detectability. A direct, *nonvalidated* transfer of Grimes's exposure parameter gives `exp(-4.09e-6 × 55,000) = 79.856%`, not `98.5%`. A human exposure parameter is not an empirical estimate for any hypothetical nonhuman population. Furthermore, independently acting human participants are not a necessary consequence of the premises, and historical record completeness changes with time.

## 4. Genomic sampling and institutional participation

A simplified per-agent probability of a detectable biological result is `f×s×g`, where `f` is deep-integration fraction, `s` is probability of relevant sampling conditional on integration, and `g` combines biological discriminability and successful surfacing/classification by the analysis pipeline. For `N` independent agents:

\[
\mathbb E[D]=Nfsg,\qquad P(D=0)=(1-fsg)^N\approx e^{-Nfsg}.
\]

| Synthetic case | `N` | `f` | `s` | `g` | Expected detections | Exact `P(D=0)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deep integration, recognizable biology | 1,000 | 0.50 | 0.20 | 0.90 | 90 | `1.10×10^-41` |
| Low integration, low recognizability | 1,000 | 0.01 | 0.20 | 0.01 | 0.02 | 98.02% |

The earlier `8.19×10^-40` figure is the **Poisson approximation** to the first zero-detection probability, not the exact independent-Bernoulli result. The distinction does not change the substantive lesson but matters for reproducibility.

Human-reference alignment and quality-control choices can affect whether anomalous biological data are noticed; it is incorrect to assume all anomalous reads are invariably discarded, or that every routine clinical encounter includes genomic sequencing. A test would audit actual raw-data retention, quality-control paths, metagenomic contamination procedures, and sampling selection before assigning `g`.

## 5. Warden vs. cohabitant and the conservation paradox

Let `r∈[0,1]` be a defined risk score with a specified spatial footprint, time horizon, and valued objective. Use a sigmoid intervention response:

\[
q_H(r,r_H)=[1+\exp(-20(r-r_H))]^{-1}.
\]

Illustrative prior distributions for the *intervention threshold*: `rW~Beta(8,2)` and `rC~Beta(3,5)`. At `r=0.5`, prior-averaged intervention probabilities are **3.511%** and **74.979%**, respectively. If an intervention were *directly and reliably observed*, it would have BF≈**21.35** for HC over HW; observed non-intervention gives BF≈**3.86** for HW over HC. **No detection of intervention is not the same observation as verified non-intervention**: the actual likelihood requires a detector for interventions and a model for ordinary events.

The premise that an intelligence *cares about climate* does not specify any threshold, lead time, technical options, cost of action, or publicly observable response. Claims that nuclear war, bioweapons, and climate disruption are all necessarily fast and irreversible are too broad. Some impacts could be local or recoverable, others globally consequential, and climate change can unfold over decades and centuries while causing persistent losses.

One coherent **additional** warden objective is preservation of the *generative capacity or option value of evolutionary processes*, rather than particular species, current ecosystem composition, or a particular climate. This is a proposed resolution of the conservation paradox, not something entailed by the four premises. It demands measurable proxies for irrecoverable loss of evolutionary opportunities and a way to distinguish that objective from self-protection by a cohabitant.

A monitoring role may be combined with either objective. Surface embedding may offer information unavailable through remote sensing, but whether it is cheapest or necessary depends on unspecified technology and operations.

## 6. Anthropic shadow and evidence of mechanisms

Condition on the existence of observers and the selection process by which events enter a dataset. A surviving civilization's sequence of near-misses is not, by itself, persuasive evidence of protection. The anthropic-shadow literature demonstrates selection biases when inferring catastrophic hazard rates from histories in which observers persist. It does not immunize **independently authenticated causal mechanisms** against analysis. However, in an adversarial model an intervention could be engineered to resemble normal human decisions, reducing mechanism detectability. Such a strategy remains hypothetical, not evidence that particular historic decisions were covertly directed.

## 7. The adversarial-detection layer (new experiment)

Let the hider select `d` before observations, while the observer has a fixed, explicitly specified experiment. The hider optimizes:

\[
d^*=\arg\min_{d\in[0.001,0.1]}
\left[D_{KL}\big(P_{1,d}(Y)\,\|\,P_0(Y)\big)
+\kappa\log(0.1/d)\right],\qquad N=1000.
\]

`P0` and `P1,d` are **prior-predictive count distributions**. The KL divergence (in nats) measures expected separation under the signal model. `kappa` is an arbitrary relative concealment-cost coefficient; lower `d` increases operational cost. This is a *single-agent best response*, not a full defender-versus-hider equilibrium, and does not imply any actual cost function for nonhuman agents. `d` cannot be chosen retrospectively after seeing `Y` without modeling the selection process.

| `kappa` | Optimal `d` (uncertain B) | Expected signal `Nd` | `KL(P1||P0)` | BF at `Y=1020` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02 | 0.01480 | 14.80 | 0.01005 | 1.0305 |
| 0.10 | 0.03340 | 33.40 | 0.05054 | 1.0408 |
| 0.50 | 0.07618 | 76.18 | 0.25600 | 0.9419 |

For the **same** cost parameters and a **known** background of 1,000, the optima are `d≈0.00448, 0.01002, 0.02249` respectively. With background uncertainty the hider can sustain a larger observable footprint at the same optimization tradeoff. Calibration of nuisance processes can therefore change both observer inference and optimal hider behavior.

KL does **not** imply that the realized Bayes factor is 1 for every observation. If the distributions are exactly identical then BF=1 on their common support. If their KL divergence is small, statistical distinguishability is bounded *on average*, but rare observations may still have large likelihood ratios. Pinsker's inequality bounds total variation by `sqrt(KL/2)`; it does not create a headcount law without additional structure.

### Why the square-root steganography analogy is limited

Cachin (1998/2004) models distinguishability using relative entropy between cover and stego distributions. Ker et al. (2008) establish and test square-root scaling under assumptions including particular detection responses and imperfect hiding. These results make useful analogies for an adversarial hiding problem; **they are not direct population laws for biology or social institutions**. Perfectly distribution-preserving hiding can have positive linear capacity, and real observation streams may be correlated, adaptive, multimodal, or distribution-shifting. To justify an `O(sqrt(H))` resident footprint against human-cover size `H`, one must prove the corresponding divergence scaling for a specific observational channel.

### Historical v1.0 proposal (implemented as a constrained toy game in §10)

Introduce observer investment `a` (sampling effort, authentication, background calibration) and a feasible concealment policy `u` with vector detectability `d_j(u,a)` over distinct channels `j`. Study an explicitly sequential game:

\[
\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\min_{u\in\mathcal U}
\big[ D_{KL}(P_{1,u,a}\|P_{0,a})+C_H(u)-C_O(a)\big],
\]

**Only as a provisional mathematical sketch**: the sign and role of hider operating cost must be specified consistently with a chosen zero-sum or general-sum utility before this becomes an actual minimax model. A preferable approach is to write separate observer and hider payoffs and solve for a best-response pair or equilibrium. Because some physical modalities are harder to manipulate than self-reports, a multidimensional frontier is more informative than a single scalar detectability `d`.

## 8. Proposed prospective protocol

1. Define evidence streams, observation windows, and a realistic null model *before* acquiring test data.
2. Independently calibrate the background distribution `B`, including nonstationarity, correlation, selection, hoaxes, instrument errors, and ordinary mechanisms.
3. Predefine the physical criteria for an authenticated event and the procedures for chain of custody, negative controls, replication, and blinded review.
4. Specify a separate sampling/access/analysis pipeline for biological, clinical, demographic, physical, and historical channels; do not assume a uniform detectability parameter across modalities.
5. Register priors over `N`, integration, observability, hider cost functions, and time-varying secrecy parameters, and specify robust alternative priors.
6. Distinguish verified event/non-event from merely detected/not-detected evidence, especially in intervention analysis.
7. Integrate over nuisance parameters and report likelihoods, Bayes factors, posterior sensitivity, and how observations were selected.
8. Assess adversarial costs as unvalidated structural constraints unless independently grounded; avoid importing human secrecy parameters as measurements of hypothetical nonhuman behavior.
9. Reserve an untouched prospective holdout sample and stop rules to prevent post-hoc selection of an apparent signature.

## 9. Limits and epistemic status

- These models do **not** show that extraterrestrial or cryptoterrestrial residents exist; none of the synthetic counts represent real resident detections.
- Neither the four premises nor the reviewed steganographic and secrecy research supply a scientifically established prior probability of nonhuman surface residency.
- A model that can replicate all observations by freely choosing stealth and intervention parameters yields little explanatory discrimination. Identifiability and out-of-sample prediction are requirements for useful claims.
- Evidence for the existence of an actor, its actions, and its objective are different evidential problems.
- No hypothesis about historical crises is validated by the mere occurrence of a near-miss or a human decision that prevented disaster.

## 10. Version 1.1: closed-form best response and cost sensitivity

**Revision scope:** The v1.0 synthetic calculations and original `verify_models.py` are preserved. This section corrects interpretation of the adversarial optima, establishes an identifiability condition for the count model, and supplies an explicitly sequential defender–attacker toy game. All parameter values remain hypothetical. None is evidence of nonhuman presence.

### 10.1 Log concealment cost: stationary solution and its domain

Let `s=Nd`, `V` be the **prior predictive count variance** under a calibrated background model, and suppose `s` is small enough for the local quadratic approximation

\[
D_{KL}(P_{1,s}\|P_0)\approx\frac{s^2}{2V}.
\]

For the v1.0 objective (constant terms omitted):

\[
J(s)=\frac{s^2}{2V}+\kappa\log(s_{max}/s),
\qquad s\in[s_{min},s_{max}],
\]

\[
\frac{dJ}{ds}=\frac{s}{V}-\frac{\kappa}{s}=0
\implies s^*_{\rm interior}=\sqrt{\kappa V}.
\]

With the hard bounds, `s* = clip(sqrt(kappa V), s_min, s_max)`. Only **if the optimum is interior** does

\[
D_{KL}^*\approx\frac{\kappa}{2}.
\]

Using `V=1000` (known background) and `V=11000` (uncertain background), the footprint ratio is `sqrt(11)≈3.3166`. **At an interior optimum under precisely this logarithmic cost and Gaussian small-signal approximation**, calibration constrains the activity footprint but does not change optimized KL. The exact Poisson and Gamma–Poisson objectives deviate slightly from the quadratic approximation; bounds and a different cost function can eliminate the invariance. KL is an expectation over observations, not a realized likelihood ratio or detection success probability.

| kappa | s*, known V (quadratic) | s*, uncertain V (quadratic) | KL* (either; quadratic) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02 | 4.4721 | 14.8324 | 0.0100 |
| 0.10 | 10.0000 | 33.1662 | 0.0500 |
| 0.50 | 22.3607 | 74.1620 | 0.2500 |

The v1.0 exact optimizer gives uncertain-background signals near 14.80, 33.40, and 76.18: these are not identical to the local approximation, especially as the signal grows.

### 10.2 Power-law operating-cost sensitivity

A plausible alternative *functional family*, not an empirically established cost model, is `C(s)=c s^{-alpha}` with positive `c` and `alpha`:

\[
J_{\alpha}(s)=\frac{s^2}{2V}+cs^{-\alpha},\qquad
s^*=\operatorname{clip}\big[(\alpha cV)^{1/(\alpha+2)}\big],
\]

\[
D_{KL}^*\approx\frac{(\alpha c)^{2/(\alpha+2)}}{2}
V^{-\alpha/(\alpha+2)}.
\]

Unlike logarithmic cost, optimized divergence falls as predictive variance rises. Example: `alpha=1`, `c=1` gives `s*=10` and `KL*=0.05` at `V=1000`, versus `s*=22.24`, `KL*=0.0224` at `V=11000`. Reducing the background uncertainty increases optimized KL by `11^(1/3)≈2.224`, subject to interior-solution and approximation conditions. Calibration's effect on *detectability* therefore depends on the shape and magnitude of operating costs.

### 10.3 Identifiability: ON/OFF is structural, not optional

For repeated independent single-channel measurements `Y_t~Poisson(B+s)` with unknown, constant `B≥0` and `s≥0`, the joint distribution depends only on `B+s`. Any feasible pair `(B',s')` with `B'+s'=B+s` has the same likelihood, even with infinite repetitions. The Fisher information for the two parameters is rank one. **No number of repeated ON-only measurements identifies the decomposition.**

A matched signal-free control can change that: `Y_on~Poisson(B+s)`, `Y_off~Poisson(B)` (equal exposure, independent counts) jointly identify the ON and OFF means and hence the contrast `s`. More generally, known relative exposure `alpha` gives a null constraint `mu_on=alpha*mu_off`. The Li–Ma (1983) ON/OFF construction is the precedent for significance under that structured null. It does **not** imply that an arbitrary chosen control is genuinely signal-free, nor that backgrounds are automatically exchangeable. Unmodeled ON–OFF shifts can mimic signal.

If a strategic hider can add `a` to the OFF observation, then the observed difference identifies `s-a` rather than `s`. At `a=s`, and with unknown common background, both observed means agree and the original signal can be absorbed into the fitted common background. Observer randomization matters only under explicit limits on manipulation.

### 10.4 Toy Stackelberg game: defender randomizes one chosen control

The defender has one ON site and two *candidate* matched OFF controls, A and B. One reference is privately sampled in each of `n=100` independent blocks. Background per site and block has a common unknown Poisson mean `B=1000` (used to generate the alternative; the null fits this nuisance). The ON site receives `s=20` extra expected counts per block. The hider can permanently contaminate **at most one** of A or B by adding a precisely matched extra mean `s` before the defender's control draws. The hider sees the defender's committed randomization probability `p=P(select A)` but not the subsequent private draws. Equal exposures and matching background are assumptions.

The defender maximizes the expected **profiled Poisson KL separation** of ON and selected OFF measurements under a common-mean null; the hider minimizes that expected separation plus a fixed operating cost for contaminating one control. We report both defender and hider utilities separately, avoiding the ambiguous sign convention in the v1.0 provisional max–min sketch.

For independent ON and OFF Poisson observations with alternative means `mu_on=B+s`, `mu_off=B+a`, the KL divergence to the closest common-mean Poisson null is

\[
D_{\rm profile}(a)=n\left[\mu_{on}\log\frac{2\mu_{on}}{\mu_{on}+\mu_{off}}
+\mu_{off}\log\frac{2\mu_{off}}{\mu_{on}+\mu_{off}}\right].
\]

This is an **information projection**, not a conventional Bayesian marginal likelihood or an out-of-sample success probability. With a clean OFF channel `a=0` it is positive, and with a perfectly contaminated channel `a=s` it is zero. Let `D0 = D_profile(0)`.

The hider's choices are `{none, contaminate A, contaminate B}`; each contamination costs `c_att`. Defender commits to `p`, hider best-responds to minimize `E[D_profile]+cost`, and only then are private control draws realized. With `c_att=0`:

\[
E[D\mid\text{attack A}]=(1-p)D_0,\qquad
E[D\mid\text{attack B}]=pD_0.
\]

Hence the follower's best-response information is `min(p,1-p)*D0`, and the leader chooses `p*=1/2`, yielding `D0/2`. Deterministic selection (`p=0` or `1`) allows a zero-information response. With an attack cost of `0.25*D0`, the defender still optimally chooses `p=1/2` and the attacker contaminates one control. With an attack cost of `0.75*D0`, the attacker optimally abstains at `p=1/2`; then expected information is the full `D0`.

**Crucial limitations:** The result fails if the hider can see the realized private assignment in time to respond, spoof both controls completely at affordable cost, affect the ON data, or exploit uncontrolled background differences. If hider contamination can be split continuously across controls, the binary action restriction also changes the equilibrium. Nothing in the original four premises establishes a bound on such abilities. Randomized controls improve identifiability only **conditional on an independently justified uncontaminated-reference or adversarial-resource assumption**.

### 10.5 What follows—and what does not

The additional work suggests a future design exercise, not a retrospective identification of covert actors. A defensible study needs: (i) a structural source of variation that separates background from candidate signal; (ii) a verified way to blind or randomize control status, if that is central to the experiment; (iii) limits on what can plausibly affect both measurement channels; (iv) competing conventional hypotheses; and (v) a range of operating-cost specifications rather than one convenient functional form.

The `verify_models_v1_1.py` script calculates the closed-form expressions and exact profiled-Poisson quantities, performs assertions, and writes `results_v1_1.json`, `cost_sensitivity.csv`, and `stackelberg_results.csv`. The original v1.0 verifier is kept unchanged; its outputs remain byte-for-byte reproducible.
